Team Daily Stress Analysis
Per-player load score vs that player's own historical average for the same session type over the last 4 weeks. 100 = normal for this session type · <100 lighter than usual · >100 heavier than usual. Always uses all drills (excl. Entire Session) — independent of the drill filter below.
How? For each player we compare today's totals against their own average across all sessions of the same type (e.g. MD-3) in the last 28 days (Entire Session excluded). Per metric: today / average × 100. Then weighted into three blocks: Volume 0.60·TD% + 0.40·Time%  ·  Intensity 0.50·HSR% + 0.35·Sprint% + 0.15·mV%  ·  Mechanical 0.40·Acc% + 0.60·Dec%. Final score = 0.35·Volume + 0.35·Intensity + 0.30·Mechanical. Why? An MD-3 shouldn't look like a match — comparing against the player's own norm for that session type gives a fairer answer to "is this heavier than usual?"
No Baseline = fewer than 2 comparable sessions in 4 weeks — no score shown.

ACWR — Acute:Chronic Workload

ACWR = what the player did in the last 7 days (acute) divided by their average week over the last 28 days (chronic). Around 1.0 the recent week matches what the body is used to.
Why? Spikes in this ratio are one of the best-documented injury predictors: building up faster than the body is prepared for (> 1.5) raises injury risk, while doing much less than usual (< 0.8) loses robustness and raises risk on the return to full load. The 0.8–1.3 band is the "sweet spot".
No baseline = fewer than 6 training days in the 28-day window — the ratio would be unreliable, so it isn't shown.
< 0.8 — underload 0.8–1.3 — sweet spot 1.3–1.5 — caution > 1.5 — high risk
Camp arrival — load differentiation

What this shows. Players join camp from different clubs, each arriving in a different training state. This card reads every player's recent load history and turns it into one clear arrival status plus a concrete, day-by-day session adjustment — so the staff can individualise the first days of camp instead of running the same plan for everyone.
How the status is decided. Five signals are combined for each player: (1) ACWR — the acute:chronic workload ratio built from the combined club + national-team load; (2) last match — minutes played and days since; (3) readiness — the morning wellness check-in; (4) CMJ trend — change in jump height versus the previous test; and (5) a return-to-play gap detector that flags a recent break in loading.
The statuses. Overloaded → taper (arrived with a load spike or a full match just played) · Fatigue → monitor (low readiness, dropping CMJ or high soreness) · Underloaded → top-up (arrived undertrained — add compensatory load) · Optimal → maintain (in the sweet spot — follow the squad plan) · RTP → progressive (returning from a lay-off — build up gradually) · Insufficient data (too few loaded days to judge — manual review, never a guess).
Reading MD-3 / MD-2 / MD-1. Each column is the suggested adjustment versus the standard squad session on that day before the next match (e.g. −30% volume on MD-3). "normal" means follow the squad plan.
Advisory. These are decision-support suggestions, not instructions — the staff sets the thresholds and can override any row, and RTP flags always need medical sign-off. Click a player's name for the full cross-module profile behind the status.

What this shows. Training Monotony & Strain (Foster) flags weeks where the pattern of load — not just the total — raises the risk of illness, injury and overreaching. Shown per player for the selected week, across the combined club + national-team load.
Daily load used here. The default metric is Eccentric Load — a weighted count of high-intensity decelerations (zone 4×1 + zone 5×2 + zone 6×3), the braking/eccentric actions most linked to muscle damage. Each day's load = the individual training drills, and on match days the 1st + 2nd half only (the Entire Session rollup and the 15-min blocks are excluded, so nothing is double-counted). Switch the metric (TD / HSR / Acc / Dec) with the pills.
The numbers. Monotony = mean daily load ÷ standard deviation across the week (7-day basis, rest days count as 0) — a high value means the player trained at almost the same load every day. Strain = weekly load × monotony — the total accumulated stress. The 7-day sparkline shows the daily loads Mon→Sun (an empty bar is a rest day). Strain vs prev compares this week's strain with the player's previous week (▲ rising = building stress, ▼ falling = deload).
Why it matters. Foster found that weeks with high monotony and high strain tend to precede illness and injury even when the weekly total looks normal — variety and well-placed rest days protect the player. Monotony is unit-independent, so the thresholds below apply to any load metric.
Advisory. Decision-support for the staff, not an automatic rule — read it alongside the ACWR and Camp arrival cards.
Monotony < 1.5 — good variation 1.5–2.0 — monitor > 2.0 — high

Explanation of metrics
  • Max Speed (Selected): highest speed reached during the selected drill(s).
  • % Max Speed: relative intensity vs the player's historical maximum.
  • Max Speed Historical: highest validated speed across the full dataset. Readings > 36.5 km/h are excluded as GPS glitches, and the chosen value must have been reached at least 3 times (within ±1.5%) — singletons aren't trusted as a real ceiling. Tooltip shows date, session type and the validation tier (validated · fallback · single reading).
  • Max Acc / Dec: peak acceleration and deceleration during the selected drill(s).
  • % Max Acc / Dec: relative intensity vs the validated historical maximum (same ≥ 3-occurrence rule as Max Speed). Historical Acc / Dec values follow the same dotted-underline cue when only a fallback could be found.
  • Sprint (>25 km/h): total distance covered above 25 km/h (Distance Zone 6).
  • Step Balance: STATSports metric quantifying how mechanical load is distributed between the left and right leg. Shown as a left / right percentage split with a flag for the dominant side (e.g. 48 / 52 R = right leg carries 4 pp more load). 49–51 balanced · 51–53.5 monitor · > 53.5 risk (possible fatigue, compensation or injury risk). Always interpret in context of speed, position and limb dominance.
Intensity colour scale (% columns): < 85% Low 85–95% Moderate > 95% High
